02 / MY STORY

Always asking why.

Technology, for me, began with curiosity — not a job title.

01

Where it started

As a child, I was fascinated by computers and how a toy light gun could interact with a screen in a duck-shooting game. I wanted to understand what happened behind the scenes. That curiosity led me to explore computer science at school and learn about web development through online resources.

02

Turning records into answers

In my family's industrial gas business, I helped organize inventory information and saw how manual tracking could make simple questions difficult to answer. Later, I independently developed a local web application to manage cylinder movements, balances and reports. It made the connection between technology and everyday business needs tangible.

03

Building my next chapter

I chose NorQuest's Machine Learning Analyst diploma because I wanted to understand patterns in data and build models that could support decisions. Moving to Edmonton also allowed me to be close to my brother. My goal is to continue my education and grow into a technical role where I can eventually lead projects.

05 / PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT

Evidence before claims.

Good results are only valuable when their limitations are understood.

Privacy by design

Confidential automotive data, business records, credentials and original client files are excluded. The inventory demo uses fictional data. Academic and API-derived material is summarized rather than redistributed.

Evaluate honestly

Training performance is not proof of future generalization. I report the recorded evaluation results, including negative test R² in the waste project, and identify possible leakage and limited sample sizes.

Understand the data

Missing values, duplicate records, imputed coordinates and sampling bias affect conclusions. I distinguish a recorded result from a proposed improvement and avoid claiming that a model is production-ready without evidence.
